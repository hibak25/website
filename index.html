Virtual Farm Study Summary

A polished webpage summary of the study on using a virtual farm for elementary science education.

Focus of the Study

The research examines whether a virtual reality farm can improve elementary science learning, especially in plant growth and poultry raising.

Sample Studied

Four 3rd-grade classes from one elementary school in Hsinchu participated, with 69 students in the experimental group and 70 in the control group.

Average Effect Size

The paper does not report a formal effect size such as Cohen’s d, but it reports a statistically significant advantage for the virtual farm group.

Further Findings

Students described the system as realistic, interesting, easy to use, and helpful for learning; the study also observed signs of discussion and peer learning.

Opening Paragraph

This study describes the design and classroom use of a virtual farm created to support elementary science education. The platform allows students to take the role of a farmer, care for crops and animals, and learn agricultural concepts through an interactive online environment.

Introduction

The study was designed to make science learning more active, practical, and motivating for young learners. To achieve this, the authors built a web-based virtual farm using virtual reality, 3D graphics, and learning activities based on real farm life.

What Is This Study About?

The study focuses on a simulated farm environment where students can learn by doing. In the virtual farm, students can grow vegetables, hatch eggs, raise poultry, and observe how living things change over time. The system also includes educational content, interactive features, and a discussion forum for sharing experiences.

What Does This Study Find?

The main outcome shows that students who used the virtual farm achieved better results than those who learned through traditional teaching.

  • Control group: pretest mean 61.23, SD 17.65; posttest mean 78.21, SD 16.31; gain 16.98.
  • Experimental group: pretest mean 58.30, SD 17.01; posttest mean 84.36, SD 11.65; gain 26.06.
  • ANCOVA: significant group difference, F = 15.87, p = .001.
  • Homogeneity test: similar regression slopes, F = 0.71, p = .40.
  • Reliability: Cronbach’s alpha = 0.94 for the achievement test.

Study Example

One example from the study is the crop-growing activity, where students prepared the soil, planted seeds, watered crops, fertilized them, removed weeds, and harvested them at the right time. Another example is the poultry activity, where students managed feed, water, cleanliness, hatching, and animal health inside the virtual hennery.

Scientific Relevance

This study is scientifically relevant because it combines educational theory with interactive technology and evaluates the outcome using a controlled pretest-posttest design. The authors measured actual learning gains and compared the experimental group with a control group rather than relying only on opinions. The added use of questionnaires, observation, and interviews gives the findings greater depth and educational value.

Conclusion

Overall, the study shows that a virtual farm can make science learning more engaging while also improving achievement in elementary students. The statistical results support the effectiveness of the method, and student feedback suggests that the system was both enjoyable and educational.

Reference

Tarng, W., Chang, M.-Y., Ou, K.-L., Yu, K.-F., & Hsieh, K.-R. (2012). The development of a virtual farm for applications in elementary science education. International Journal of Distance Education Technologies, 10(2), 1–16.
